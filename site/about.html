About Baltic Lanes

What this is

Baltic Lanes is an animated map of twenty years of shipping in the Baltic Sea (2006–2026). Every dot of light on the sea is an aggregate of thousands of position reports — never an individual vessel. There is no vessel lookup, no tracking, and no way to identify a boat.

The data

AIS (Automatic Identification System) is the radio system ships use to broadcast their position, course and speed. The Danish Maritime Authority publishes the AIS traffic it receives for Danish waters as open data at aisdata.ais.dk — daily files back to 2006, roughly 3 TB in total, covering the Baltic Sea and the Danish straits. We publish aggregates only (density grids, counts, flows); raw AIS files are never redistributed.

Method

Messages are validated against the Baltic bounding box, de-duplicated, classified into six vessel groups (tanker, cargo, passenger/ferry, cruise, fishing, pleasure craft) and aggregated onto a ~1 km grid, per month and per group. The full methodology, QA numbers and license notes are documented in the project repository and land here with the data release.

Known limitations

Class B (mostly leisure) transponders are underrepresented — not every sailboat carries one. Coverage reflects the Danish Maritime Authority's receiving area. Counts are message counts, not unique ships, unless stated otherwise.
